01│The problem, in one seller's words

“My ads stopped working.”

A Brazilian seller opens Meta Ads Manager and watches ROAS fall from 2.4 to 0.6.

Ads Manager tells them what happened. It cannot tell them where — and it has never seen their product page, their margin, their stock, or their delivery promise.

So they do the only thing the dashboard suggests. They change the creative.

Most of the time the creative was fine. The money was leaking somewhere Meta cannot see.

This is the misattribution the whole product exists to correct.

06│How we know it's right — do not cut for time

We built a causal simulator.

Sample a real Brazilian store from 97,000 Olist orders, inject one fault, run 30 days of funnel maths. The label is decided first — it is never derived from the metrics.

400 campaigns. 100 held out. Nine fault kinds.

Names the right cause59%
False alarms on 25 healthy campaigns12%
Names the change point on detected breaks100%
Change point within ±1 day — sudden breaks93%
Change point within ±1 day — gradual ramps0%
Chance floor25%

pnpm sim:backtest

Volunteer the 0%. Quote sudden and gradual separately and never average them. One number over two populations that behave nothing alike would flatter the gradual case and understate the sudden one.

And volunteer this: thin_pdp and price_too_high score 0%. It is a benchmark-width problem — −0.86σ against a 4.5–12% IQR — and we deliberately did not fix it by moving the −1.0σ threshold. Moving a threshold until your own test passes is how you stop being able to trust it.

pnpm sim:allocator

Measured against curves the engine never sees, over 200 simulated accounts. Greedy is what sellers actually do, and it returns less than nothing. One reason, and a seller can hold it: the best product fills up.

Do not claim 94%. optimization.md imagines it; we get 71.4%, and the reason is a 14% median error in the marginal return.

08│What we deliberately did not build

Saying no is the slide.

  • No deep learning. On this exact problem, published: linear regression R² 0.74, LSTM 0.56. Simple beat deep, explainable beat both.
  • No bandits, no Bayesian optimization. They need live evaluation loops. A half-built GP that does not converge on stage is worse than a solver that always does.
  • No response curve for a flat budget. A campaign held at one budget contains no evidence about any other budget. The fit refuses rather than returning confident nonsense.
  • No Meta Ads MCP. Not built, on purpose.

Judges reward a team that knows what it chose not to build.

A2│Appendix — questions, and the honest answers
“Isn't this a dashboard?”
A dashboard shows ROAS is 0.6. Mazal says the ad worked and the product page didn't, and hands you actions tagged with who can perform them. We built the opposite of a dashboard: it shows one thing, the leak.
“How do you know it isn't hallucinating?”
The model never touches arithmetic. Every finding carries the rule id, the observed value, the reference, and the sample size. Audit any number on screen right now.
“Doesn't Meta's own AI do this?”
Meta optimises inside Meta. It cannot see your margin, your stock, your delivery ETA, or your product page — and it is not incentivised to tell you not to spend.
“Where are your benchmarks from?”
Computed, not quoted. Per-category medians and quartiles from Olist, in BRL. Every reference number prints its sample size.
“What if it's wrong?”
It proposes, you approve, and the receipt names which mode it ran in. On thin data it says it cannot predict yet and names the number to instrument first.
“Can it spend my money?”
Slide 5's sentence, exactly.
“How is this a business?”
Zero integration to diagnose — a CSV and a two-minute form. One OAuth to execute. Every competitor needs OAuth before it can say hello.

Every number in this deck is regenerated by a command, and the command is named beside it. If a figure is not reproducible from the repo, it does not go on a slide.
