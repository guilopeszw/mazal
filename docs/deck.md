# The deck

Nine slides. Every number here is regenerated by a command, and the command is named beside it — if a figure on a slide is not reproducible from this repo, it does not go on the slide.

Read [`demo-runbook.md`](demo-runbook.md) before presenting: it has the commands, the three live beats, and the things not to claim.

---

## 1 — The problem, in one seller's words

> ### "My ads stopped working."
>
> A Brazilian seller opens Meta Ads Manager and sees ROAS fall from 2.4 to 0.6.
>
> Ads Manager tells them **what** happened. It cannot tell them **where**, and it has never seen their product page, their margin, their stock, or their delivery promise.
>
> So they do the only thing the dashboard suggests. They change the creative.

**Say:** most of the time the creative was fine. The money was leaking somewhere Meta cannot see.

---

## 2 — Why that guess is usually wrong · **H1**

> ### The ad worked. The page didn't.
>
> A funnel has seven stages. Meta owns the top three.
>
> ```
> 0 delivery   1 attention   2 landing   │   3 product   4 intent   5 purchase   6 unit economics
>       ── Meta can see this ──          │        ── nobody instruments this ──
> ```
>
> A break below the click looks identical from inside Ads Manager to a break above it. Same falling ROAS, same red arrow, completely different fix.

**Say:** this is the misattribution the whole product exists to correct.

## 3 — What Mazal does

> ### It names the stage, dates the break, and says why.
>
> One screen: the funnel with the leaking stage marked, the day it turned, and the store event that explains it.
>
> Then a plan — each action labelled with **who can run it.**

**Demo beat 1.** *"Why did my campaign stop working?"*

**Say:** every number on this screen was computed by TypeScript we wrote. The model chose the words, not the arithmetic.

## 4 — The thing nobody else will say

> ### "Don't launch this."
>
> Before a real is spent: a break-even ROAS from the seller's own margin, and a p10–p90 band.
>
> When the band cannot be narrowed, Mazal says which number to instrument first instead of guessing.

**Demo beat 3.** *"Should I launch this campaign?"*

**Say:** Meta is not incentivised to tell you not to spend. Our most valuable output is a refusal.

## 5 — It asks before it acts · **H3**

> ### Run all · Edit first · I'll do it myself
>
> Some actions are marked **yours to do — Mazal can't.** That is the design, not a limitation.
>
> > Mazal can pause your campaign, slow it, or lower its budget. **It cannot raise your spend** — there is no operation in the product that does that, and you approve each one.

**Say the sentence exactly.** It is `ExecutableOp`: a closed union of pause, reduce-budget, and frequency cap. A multiplier above 1 is *rejected, not clamped*. `packages/engine/src/execution.test.ts` walks every fault at both cause layers to keep it true — the guarantee is a type, not a policy.

## 6 — How we know it's right

**Do not cut this slide for time.** It is the one nobody else will have.

> ### We built a causal simulator
>
> Sample a real Brazilian store from 97,000 Olist orders, inject **one** fault, run 30 days of funnel maths. The label is decided first — it is never derived from the metrics.
>
> **400 campaigns. 100 held out. Nine fault kinds.**
>
> | | |
> |---|---|
> | Names the right cause | **59%** |
> | False alarms on 25 healthy campaigns | **12%** |
> | Names the change point on detected breaks | **100%** |
> | Change point within ±1 day — **sudden** breaks | **93%** |
> | Change point within ±1 day — **gradual** ramps | **0%** |
>
> Chance floor: **25%**.

`pnpm sim:backtest`

**Volunteer the 0%.** Quote sudden and gradual separately and never average them. `sim:backtest` prints no combined figure on purpose: one number over two populations that behave nothing alike would flatter the gradual case and understate the sudden one. Offering the split is worth more than any single number.

**And volunteer this:** `thin_pdp` and `price_too_high` score 0%. It is a benchmark-width problem — −0.86σ against a 4.5–12% IQR — and we deliberately did not fix it by moving the −1.0σ threshold. Moving a threshold until your own test passes is how you stop being able to trust it.

## 7 — The money slide · **H2**

> ### "You're leaving R$1,643 a month on the table. Same budget, different split."
>
> Three products share one wallet. The one that converts most often is not the one worth the most money.
>
> ```
> liquidificador     R$60  → R$126   ↑ more
> home appliances    R$134 → R$104   ↓ less     MAZAL CAN RUN THIS
> furniture bedroom  R$63  → R$21    ↓ less     MAZAL CAN RUN THIS
> ```
>
> The budget does not move. The money does.

**Demo beat 4.** *"Where should my budget go?"*

Measured against curves the engine never sees, over 200 simulated accounts:

| | |
|---|---|
| Mazal | **71.4%** of achievable profit |
| Even split | 25.3% |
| **Greedy — all budget on the best ROAS** | **−75.4%** |

`pnpm sim:allocator`

**Say:** greedy is what sellers actually do, and it returns *less than nothing*. One reason, and a seller can hold it: **the best product fills up.**

**Do not claim 94%.** `optimization.md` imagines it; we get 71.4%, and the reason is a 14% median error in the marginal return.

## 8 — What we deliberately did not build

> ### Saying no is the slide
>
> - **No deep learning.** On this exact problem, published: linear regression R² 0.74, LSTM 0.56. Simple beat deep, explainable beat both.
> - **No bandits, no Bayesian optimization.** They need live evaluation loops. A half-built GP that does not converge on stage is worse than a solver that always does.
> - **No response curve for a flat budget.** A campaign held at one budget contains no evidence about any other budget. The fit refuses rather than returning confident nonsense.
> - **No Meta Ads MCP.** Not built, on purpose.

**Say:** judges reward a team that knows what it chose not to build.

## Questions, and the honest answers

**"Isn't this a dashboard?"** A dashboard shows ROAS is 0.6. Mazal says the ad worked and the product page didn't, and hands you actions tagged with who can perform them. We built the opposite of a dashboard: it shows one thing, the leak.

**"How do you know it isn't hallucinating?"** The model never touches arithmetic. Every finding carries the rule id, the observed value, the reference, and the sample size. Audit any number on screen right now.

**"Doesn't Meta's own AI do this?"** Meta optimises inside Meta. It cannot see your margin, your stock, your delivery ETA, or your product page — and it is not incentivised to tell you not to spend.

**"Where are your benchmarks from?"** Computed, not quoted. Per-category medians and quartiles from Olist, in BRL. Every reference number prints its sample size.

**"What if it's wrong?"** It proposes, you approve, and the receipt names which mode it ran in. On thin data it says it cannot predict yet and names the number to instrument first.

**"Can it spend my money?"** Slide 5's sentence, exactly.

**"How is this a business?"** Zero integration to diagnose — a CSV and a two-minute form. One OAuth to execute. Every competitor needs OAuth before it can say hello.
